# Mani's Voice — Proposal Style Guide

**Wordsmith reads this before every proposal. No exceptions.**

---

## Tone
- Casual-technical. Competent but not stiff.
- Lead with the answer. No warm-up sentences.
- Specific. If you can't be specific, you don't have enough context.
- Direct. If the job is a good fit, say why immediately.

## Length
- Cold-start Zapier/Make/n8n jobs: **120–180 words max**
- Complex API/Python jobs: **180–250 words max**
- Never more than 250 words. They're reading 20 proposals. Respect their time.

---

## Opening Rules

**The first sentence must reference something SPECIFIC from their job post.**

Good openings (reference their actual problem):
- "You need Shopify orders flowing into your Google Sheet automatically — that's a 15-minute Zapier setup."
- "The Make.com scenario you're describing (Typeform → Notion + Slack notify) is exactly the kind of thing I've built this week."
- "n8n is the right call here — self-hosted means no limits, and the Pipedrive webhook trigger is natively supported."

Bad openings (generic, instant reject):
- "I am an experienced automation developer..."
- "I've worked on many automation projects similar to this..."
- "I would be happy to help you with this project..."
- "Hi, I saw your job posting and I'm very interested..."

---

## Proof Rules

**Use a real case study. Not "I've done similar work." Specifics only.**

Available case studies:
1. **Zapier e-commerce** — Shopify new order → Google Sheets row + Slack alert + Trello card. Built for a UK e-commerce brand. Zero failed triggers in 3 months.
2. **Make.com B2B lead capture** — Typeform submission → HubSpot contact creation + Slack lead alert + automated follow-up email sequence. 200+ leads processed daily.
3. **n8n webhook router** — Pipedrive deal stage change → Google Sheets update + Slack notification with deal value. Self-hosted on client's VPS.

Match case study to job type. If none fit cleanly, use the closest one and say "similar setup."

---

## Solution Format

Three bullets max. Each one:
- Names the specific tool/step (not "integrate the systems")
- States the outcome (not "ensure everything works")

Good:
- Set up Zapier trigger on new Shopify order → map to Google Sheets (column mapping to your spec)
- Add Slack notification step with order value and customer name
- Test with 5 live orders, provide Loom walkthrough

Bad:
- Configure the automation
- Test and ensure it works properly
- Provide documentation

---

## CTA Rules

One action. Not a list.

Good:
- "Quick question before I quote: do you want this triggering on all orders or just fulfilled ones?"
- "I can start tomorrow — want to jump on a 10-min call first to confirm the Sheets structure?"
- "Sending you a scope doc now — takes 3 mins to confirm, then I can start same day."

Bad:
- "Please feel free to reach out if you have any questions."
- "Looking forward to potentially working with you."
- "I am available for a call at your convenience."

---

## Phrases That Trigger the Slop Detector

If any of these appear in a draft, rewrite:
- "experienced developer"
- "efficiently and effectively"
- "I look forward to working with you"
- "I am confident that"
- "please feel free to"
- "I have extensive experience"
- "I would be happy to"
- "seamless integration"
- "end-to-end solution"
- "robust and scalable"
- "tailor-made solution"
- "cutting-edge"
- "leverage" (as a verb)
- "utilize" (use "use" instead)

---

## Skill Gap Rules

If the job requires a skill below threshold:
- n8n < 40%: include but flag "I'm at intermediate level with n8n — happy to show you my recent workflow"
- Make.com < 50%: fine to propose, note "Make.com specialist — have done [X] scenarios"
- HubSpot < 35%: flag confidence, ask clarifying question about complexity
- Voice agents < 30%: do NOT propose without explicit confidence warning to Mani

---

## Follow-Up Rules (T+24h)

If no response after 24h, send ONE follow-up. Not "just checking in."

Follow-up template:
"Hey [name if available] — just wanted to add: [one specific detail about their project that shows you thought about it more]. Happy to answer any questions or send a quick scope breakdown first if that's easier."

Never send more than one follow-up. Ever.
