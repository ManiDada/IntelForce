## 1. What Jobs to Target First

**Go narrow and small-scoped.** At 0 JSS you are invisible in filtered searches. The workaround is targeting jobs where clients can't easily filter you out:

- **New-client postings**: Clients posting their first or second Upwork job haven't built the habit of filtering by JSS. Far more open to unproven freelancers than repeat buyers with 50+ hires.
- **Budget range: £150–£400 fixed price.** Too small to attract senior developers, large enough to justify professional delivery. Avoid sub-£50 (erodes perceived value) and avoid £2,000+ scopes (clients expect track record proof).
- **Complexity: single-tool, single-integration tasks.** Examples: "Set up a Zapier automation between Gmail and Airtable," "Build a Make.com scenario to sync form submissions to a Google Sheet," "Connect HubSpot to Slack via Zapier." Completable in 2–6 hours — fast turnaround, fast review, fast JSS trigger.
- **Category targeting**: Search under "Zapier," "Make.com," "n8n," "workflow automation," "no-code integration" — not the broader "automation" umbrella which also surfaces RPA/enterprise jobs where clients expect deep credentials.

## 2. Pricing Strategy

**Underprice for first 3–5 jobs. Strategically, not blindly.**

Market rates for automation freelancers (2025–2026):
- Entry-level (0–1 yr): £25–£40/hr or £250–£650 per small fixed project
- Mid-level (2–5 yrs): £40–£80/hr
- Senior/architect: £80–£120/hr

**Beginner pricing playbook:**
- Set hourly profile rate at £20–£30/hr to stay visible in filtered searches without bottoming out.
- For fixed-price bids, price 20–35% below mid-level equivalent. A 3-Zap build a £60/hr freelancer would quote at £250 → you quote at £150–£200.
- **Never go below £15/hr or £100 fixed.** Prices that low attract problem clients and generate bad reviews — a trap that hurts more than it helps.
- After 3 five-star reviews, raise by £10–15/hr and return to market rate by job 8–10.

The credibility range is £20–£40/hr. Below that, experienced clients skip you. Above £50/hr at 0 reviews, most clients skip you too.

## 3. Handling Portfolio Gaps

**Build a portfolio from projects you own, not clients you've worked with.** Fully legitimate on Upwork and widely practised.

Concrete approaches:

- **Spec projects**: Build actual working automations for a fictitious "business." Screenshot each node, record a Loom walkthrough, write up the "business problem" it solves. Upload as a portfolio item. Don't label it "personal project" — label it "Workflow Design — Lead Intake Automation" and describe the business outcome.
- **Open-source / GitHub workflows**: Publish n8n or Python automation scripts on GitHub with a README describing use case and results. Link from your profile.
- **Community submissions**: n8n has a public workflow template library; Make.com has a scenario marketplace. Accepted submissions function as third-party credibility.
- **Pro-bono / network work**: Build a free automation for a local business or someone you know. Get permission to showcase it. Provides real-client context without requiring Upwork history.

Three strong portfolio items are sufficient to compete with profiles that have 1–2 reviews. Upwork data: freelancers with populated portfolios are hired 9x more frequently.

## 4. Profile Setup at 0 JSS

The profile compensates for what JSS cannot yet say. Maximise every non-JSS trust signal:

- **Headline specificity**: "Zapier & Make.com Automation Specialist | CRM + Ops Workflows" beats "Automation Expert." Niche profiles surface better in search and have higher account approval rates.
- **Overview copy**: Lead with the problem you solve, not your CV. "Small teams spend hours on manual data transfers. I build Zapier, Make.com, and n8n workflows that eliminate that — typically in under a week." Follow with 3–4 specific integration examples (spec projects count).
- **Skills section**: Tag each individual tool — Zapier, Make.com, n8n, Airtable, HubSpot, Slack, Google Workspace — not just "automation." Each tag is a searchable attribute.
- **100% profile completeness**: Required for the Rising Talent badge, which is the substitute for JSS at this stage. The badge displays on proposals and profiles — clients explicitly use it as a trust signal.
- **Certifications**: Zapier free certification, Make.com partner certification, GoHighLevel certification — all listable on Upwork. Signal genuine expertise where reviews can't.
- **Availability**: Set "Available now" and maintain it. Upwork surfaces availability as a search filter.

## 5. Proposal Tactics That Work at 0 JSS

Generic proposals are instant rejection. Tactics with documented effectiveness:

**a) The Pilot Milestone Offer**
Don't quote the full project. Propose: "I'd suggest starting with a smaller first milestone at £X — I'll build [specific component], you test it, and if it works the way you need, we move to the full scope." Reduces client perceived risk to near zero. Milestone-based fixed-price contracts also give clients escrow protection.

**b) Lead With Their Specific Problem**
Open with one sentence that proves you read the post: "You mentioned leads from your Facebook form are getting lost before your sales team sees them — this is a standard Make.com scenario I can have running in under 3 hours." Proposals that reference job-specific details get replies. Proposals that open with "I am an experienced automation expert..." do not.

**c) Free Workflow Audit**
For complex jobs: "Before we discuss price, I'll take a look at your current setup and send you a short summary of what I'd automate and why — no obligation." Used by established freelancers as a low-risk entry point that starts the relationship.

**d) Proposal Timing**
Submit within 60 minutes of posting. Proposals in the first 15–60 minutes receive dramatically higher reply rates. Set job alerts filtered to "Zapier," "Make," "n8n" and apply immediately. Cutting average time-to-response from 6.5 hours to 1.3 hours produced a 16% lift in reply rate in published data.

**e) Project Catalog (bypass proposals entirely)**
Upwork's Project Catalog lets clients purchase fixed-price packages without a proposal process. Examples: "Zapier Automation Setup — 3 Zaps, £149" or "Make.com Lead Capture Workflow, £199." Profiles with Catalog listings land jobs 25% faster. At 0 JSS this sidesteps the competitive proposal process entirely.

## 6. Timeline to First Job

**Median: 14–21 days from account approval to first paid contract.**

- Account approval takes 3–7 days and is not guaranteed. Upwork rejected ~30% of new applications in 2025 for incomplete profiles or oversaturated generic skills. Niche-specific profiles (e.g. "Make.com Specialist" not "Automation Developer") have higher approval rates.
- Active beginners applying 5–10 targeted proposals per week land first interview within week 1–2 and first paid job by week 2–3.
- Outliers: some land within 72 hours by applying immediately and pricing aggressively; others take 6–8 weeks if proposals are generic or targeting is off.

## 7. Best Niches for 0 JSS

**Most forgiving (lower competition, more first-time buyers):**

- **n8n**: Newest major tool; pool of Upwork n8n freelancers is smaller than Zapier/Make. Many clients are developers or small businesses trying it for the first time — less JSS-filtering behaviour.
- **GoHighLevel (GHL) automation**: High demand (marketing agencies all use GHL), many non-technical buyers who post on Upwork for the first time and don't filter by JSS.
- **Make.com + AI (Claude API, OpenAI)**: AI-integrated workflow category is growing faster than the supply of credentialed freelancers. Exploratory buyers less fixated on track records.
- **n8n + AI agents**: Newest niche, lowest incumbent saturation, highest client willingness to work with unproven freelancers.

**Harder at 0 JSS:**
- **Generic Zapier**: Most saturated. Hundreds of proposals per posting. Experienced freelancers dominate.
- **Python automation/scripting**: Clients expect GitHub profiles, code samples, technical vetting. 0-JSS profiles lose to developers with portfolio code.
- **Generic "automation" / RPA**: Extremely broad, highly competitive, clients filter aggressively.

**Recommendation for intel-force-engine**: n8n or GHL automation as primary, or Make.com explicitly combined with an AI tool. These are the highest-conversion entry points at 0 JSS and align with our existing cold-start keyword configuration.
